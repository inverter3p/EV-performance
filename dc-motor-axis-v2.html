DC Motor Dynamic Equations

These equations are used by the simulation and link voltage, current, torque, speed, and load.

Electrical equation:
Vt = R ia + L dia/dt + eb, where eb = Ke ωm

Torque equation:
Te = Kt ia

Mechanical equation:
J dωm/dt = Te − TL − Bωm

Power calculation:
Pe,in = Vt ia, Pdev = Teωm ≈ eb ia, Pout = TL|ωm|
η ≈ Pout / Pe,in for motoring operation

TLMechanical load torque opposing motion.N·mRepresents external load such as belt load, fan load, pump load, or mechanical resistance.

BViscous friction coefficient.N·m·s/radSpeed-dependent torque loss. It increases as speed increases.

Pe,inElectrical input power from the source.WComputed as Vt × ia. This includes useful output, copper loss, transient energy storage, and mechanical losses.
PdevDeveloped electromechanical conversion power inside the motor.WComputed as Te × ωm. In an ideal SI model, this is approximately equal to back-EMF power eb × ia.
PoutUseful mechanical output power delivered to the external load.WComputed as TL × |ωm| in this simple constant-load model.
ηApproximate motoring efficiency.%Computed as Pout / Pe,in × 100 when Pe,in is positive. It is not shown for braking/regenerative conditions.
Key idea for students: voltage does not directly set speed instantly. Voltage creates current; current creates torque; net torque accelerates inertia; increasing speed creates back-EMF that reduces current until torque balances load and friction.

Power idea: electrical input power is not equal to useful mechanical output power. Some power is lost as copper loss R ia2, some is lost as viscous/friction loss Bωm2, and during acceleration some energy is temporarily stored in the magnetic field and rotor inertia.
